## 📌 Project Introduction

Social media success isn't determined by posting more content—it's determined by posting the **right content, on the right platform, at the right time**.

My goal for this project was to analyze historical social media performance data and identify the factors influencing:

* Audience engagement
* Visibility
* Follower growth

Using **Microsoft Excel** as the primary analytics tool, raw performance data was transformed into an interactive business dashboard that enables marketers and decision-makers to:

* Monitor KPIs
* Compare platform performance
* Identify trends
* Make data-driven marketing decisions

In this project, I demonstrated how Excel can be used not only for calculations but also as a complete **business intelligence platform**.

# 📊 Executive Summary of Results

This past year, we published **450 posts** across Instagram, TikTok, Twitter/X, and Facebook.

Those posts generated:

* **13.2M impressions**
* **9.5M unique users reached**
* **870K total engagements**
* **10,000 new followers**
* **$117,573 total spend**

At a headline level, those are solid numbers. But once you dig into the data, a sharper story emerges: a small number of platforms, content types, and campaign strategies are responsible for most of that performance. The rest is background noise.

### Three patterns are consistent throughout the entire dataset:

1. **TikTok leads on engagement rate and isn't particularly close**
2. **Video content outperforms every other format**
3. **Posts published in the afternoon outperform every other time slot**

None of these are marginal differences—they're significant gaps that should directly shape how we allocate time and budget going forward.

# 🔍 What the Data Actually Shows

## 1. TikTok Leads — and It's Not Particularly Close

TikTok's engagement rate across **124 posts is 8.97%**.

| Platform      | Posts | Engagement Rate |
| ------------- | ----: | --------------: |
| **TikTok**    |   124 |       **8.97%** |
| **Instagram** |   129 |       **6.26%** |
| **Twitter/X** |   118 |       **3.86%** |
| **Facebook**  |    79 |       **3.46%** |

TikTok is generating more than **2.5× the engagement of Facebook and Twitter/X**, and it's doing it at meaningful scale—124 posts is a real sample size, not a handful of outliers.

Instagram is a solid second and sits above the **6.61% global average**. Facebook and Twitter/X are both below it.

Worth noting: our overall account engagement rate is **6.61%**, which matches the global benchmark exactly.

There's nothing wrong with that—but it also means we're not beating it yet.

Leaning harder into TikTok and Instagram is the clearest path to changing that.

### What this means in practice

* TikTok should be the **first priority** for content investment and budget.
* Instagram should be the **second priority**.
* Facebook and Twitter/X should be maintained but not resourced heavily until we see evidence that the returns justify it.

---

## 2. Video Is the Format That Moves People

Video posts averaged a **9.35% engagement rate** across all platforms—the highest of any format.

| Content Format | Engagement Rate |
| -------------- | --------------: |
| **Video**      |       **9.35%** |
| **Carousel**   |           6.10% |
| **Image**      |           4.88% |
| **Text**       |           2.85% |

The video advantage holds across platforms.

* TikTok: **14.03%**
* Instagram: **5.75%**
* Twitter/X video: **3.46%**
* Twitter/X text: **4.15%**

Every one of our top viral posts is either a **TikTok video or an Instagram carousel**.

The format and platform combination is doing most of the work.

### What this means in practice

The content calendar should shift to **video-first**.

* Carousels work well for product storytelling.
* Images and text have a place but shouldn't be anchoring campaigns.

---

## 3. Campaigns Are Not Created Equal

### 🏆 Summer Vibes

**Summer Vibes** produced:

* **269,120 total engagements**
* **8.08% engagement rate**
* **$0.10 CPE**

It was our **most efficient campaign of the year**.

### 👥 Spring Launch

**Spring Launch** generated the most follower growth:

* **2,833 new followers**

### 📉 Back to School

At the other end, **Back to School** produced:

* **50,982 engagements**
* **5.18% engagement rate**

This was well below average.

Both high performers shared the same profile:

* Strong seasonal relevance
* Video-forward content
* Clear visual storytelling

### Performance Decline

One honest note worth raising: impressions and reach are both down meaningfully compared to July peaks:

* **Impressions:** ↓ 37.2%
* **Reach:** ↓ 41.3%

That's not alarming on its own; seasonal patterns explain some of it. But it's worth watching heading into Q4 planning.

### Cost Efficiency

Total spend across campaigns was **$117,573** at a **$0.13 CPE**.

Summer Vibes was the most efficient at roughly **$0.10 CPE**, meaning it delivered more engagement per dollar than any other campaign.

### What this means in practice

Future campaigns should be built around the **Summer Vibes model**:

> **Seasonal hook + Video content + Single clear visual identity**

We have a proven template. Use it.

## 4. When You Post Matters More Than We've Been Treating It

Posting time has a significant relationship with engagement.

| Posting Time  | Engagement Rate |
| ------------- | --------------: |
| **Afternoon** |       **7.47%** |
| **Evening**   |       **7.15%** |
| **Morning**   |           5.69% |
| **Midday**    |           5.53% |

That's a nearly **2-percentage-point gap** between our best and worst posting windows—on the same content.

### What this means in practice

**Default to afternoon publishing** unless there's a specific reason not to.

This is a **low-effort, no-cost optimisation**.

---

## 5. Boosting Works — But Only If You're Selective

Boosted posts averaged:

* **38,217 reach**
* **3,542 engagements per post**

Organic posts averaged:

* **13,930 reach**
* **1,247 engagements per post**

That's roughly a **2.7× lift in reach and engagement from boosting**.

### Cost Comparison

| Post Type |   CPE |
| --------- | ----: |
| Organic   | $0.12 |
| Boosted   | $0.14 |

The cost difference is small, while the reach multiplier is significant.

But boosting a weak post just amplifies mediocrity.

Three of our viral posts hit **over 10,000 engagements with no boost at all**.

### What this means in practice

**Publish first, boost selectively.**

1. Give posts **24–48 hours organically**.
2. Identify what's already gaining traction.
3. Put budget behind those posts.
4. Stop boosting posts that aren't moving.

---

## 6. One Number to Watch

Our follower conversion rate sits at **0.11%**.

With **870K engagements** across the year, we converted roughly **10,031 followers**.

The engagement quality score is **1,547,421**—a strong signal that interactions are substantive, not just passive impressions.

That said, the conversion ratio still suggests many people are engaging with individual posts without committing to following the account long-term.

Stronger profile-level CTAs and content that explicitly rewards following would likely move that number.

Examples include:

* Exclusive previews
* Series content
* Behind-the-scenes content

# 💡 Recommendations

These follow directly from the patterns in the data.

## 1. Invest More in TikTok

Scale content volume and production quality on the platform that's already outperforming everything else.

## 2. Make Video the Default

Reorient the content calendar around **video-first storytelling**, with carousels and images in a supporting role.

## 3. Replicate the Summer Vibes Blueprint

**Seasonal theme + Strong visuals + Video format**

That combination works.

Build Q2 campaigns around it.

## 4. Lock in Afternoon Publishing

Standardise the posting schedule.

It costs nothing and the engagement data is clear.

## 5. Boost Based on Performance, Not Plan

Shift from pre-scheduled boosting to a **promote-what's-working model**.

It's more efficient, and the data supports it.

## 6. Set a Follower Conversion Target

Aim to lift the **0.06% rate** meaningfully over the next two quarters.

Track it as a KPI alongside engagement rate.

The brand is performing in line with industry benchmarks:

> **A small number of decisions—platform, format, timing—account for most of the results.**

The opportunity isn't to do more.

**It's to do more of what already works.**
